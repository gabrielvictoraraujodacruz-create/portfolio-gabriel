About me

From street sales to code, always solving problems

I started out selling açaí on the street at 15. After that came a payroll loan company, an artisanal açaí shop and a restaurant, where I began as a cashier and became assistant manager.

Those jobs taught me how to serve people well, organize inventory, deal with suppliers and keep daily operations running. They also showed me how much time is lost on tasks that could be simpler.

Today I bring that perspective to technology. I am at the start of my Software Engineering degree and still have a lot to learn, but I come with an advantage: I know firsthand the problems a system needs to solve.

    Case study

    This portfolio

    The problem
    The final project for my Front-End course asked for a portfolio. I wanted a site that showed who I am beyond grades: my path since age 15, my work at the office and what I study.
    The decisions
    I used Stitch, an AI design tool, only to try layouts. The final code is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with no framework, so I understand every line. I built it in Portuguese and English, replaced percentage bars with “hands-on, academic or learning” and pull live data from GitHub and dev.to.
    What went wrong
    Stitch added made-up numbers to the screens, like requests per second and percentages, and I had to remove all of them. One photo showed office information in the background and came off the site. On the first deploy, GitHub Pages sat in the queue for 14 minutes.
    What I would do differently
    I would keep the text in a single data file. Today every change has to be made twice, once per language.
    Case study

    Document generation

    The problem
    Every new client needs a document kit: a contract, a power of attorney and declarations. Putting it together by hand took time and left room for typos.
    The decisions
    I split the roles. AI only talks and gathers the client's data. A Python script fills in the office's fixed template, creates the folder and sends it to print. The result is the same every time and takes seconds.
    What went wrong
    My first attempt let AI edit the documents on the fly. It was slow, up to 20 minutes for 3 documents, and it made mistakes: it sent jobs to the wrong printer and produced a contract with missing data. Later I moved the system to another computer without standardizing first, and the output came out different there.
    What I would do differently
    I would standardize everything before moving to other machines and test with a real case before trusting it. I learned that for repetitive work, a script beats AI.
    Case study

    Publication tracking

    The problem
    Following court notices across several courts meant opening one site after another, every day.
    The decisions
    I found out that Brazil's National Council of Justice offers public APIs: one query covers several courts, with no password and no page scraping. I built it in three steps: a script collects, a filter with written rules picks what matters, and AI only writes the final summary.
    What went wrong
    Court notices carry a standard footer that repeats generic deadlines, and the filter started finding deadlines that did not belong to the case: 19 of 55 notices had more than one deadline in the text. I also learned that one of the APIs updates about 22 days late, so it cannot tell what happened yesterday. And the same notice showed up twice.
    What I would do differently
    I would measure the limits of each data source before designing the system, not after.
